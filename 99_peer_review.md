:::{note} Reviewer Comment 1.28
Even though many troubleshooting points were mentioned throughout the article, they should be in the troubleshooting guide specifically.  I suggest additional/duplicate troubleshooting points should include:
- Crystallization failure for whatever reason (remelt…)
- Alternating colors on sections while microtoming (slow down)
- Crud all over the grid (bad water quality/handling/fingerprints/not cleaning boat, etc.)
- Sulfur smokes or catches on fire (don’t heat so much nor so fast!)
- Sulfur becomes orange (too hot -- assuming you’re not following the polymerization protocol).

:::{tip} Author Response
*The authors agree with the addition of more specific information to be added to troubleshooting guide, additions are shown below:*

*Added content for Troubleshooting Guide*

*Crystallization failure*

- *Issue: Sample fails to crystallize properly (e.g., incomplete, uneven  crystallization), or crystallizes too quickly within a couple of seconds before proper nucleation can occur.*
- *Cause: Can result from incorrect cooling rate (too fast or too slow), contamination, or insufficient nucleation time.*
- *Fix: Remelt the sample and repeat the crystallization step, adjusting the cooling rate so nucleation has time to occur properly; follow the specified temperature ramp and hold times.*

*Sample cracks or plucks during ultramicrotoming*

- *Issue: Sample cracks, chips, or plucks out of the block face while sectioning.*
- *Cause: Often caused by residual stress from improper crystallization (including overly rapid crystallization), embedding issues, or unsuitable cutting temperature/speed.*
- *Fix: Remelt and recrystallize the sample with a controlled cooling rate before re-attempting sectioning; consider adjusting knife angle, cutting speed, or section thickness if the issue persists.*

*Sulfur smokes or catches fire*

- *Issue: Sulfur begins smoking or ignites during heating.*
- *Cause: Heating too much or too fast*
- *Fix: Reduce heating rate and closely monitor temperature; remove from heat immediately if smoking occurs and allow to cool before reassessing.*

*Sulfur becomes orange*

- *Issue: Sulfur turns orange during heating.*
- *Cause: Sample is too hot — this applies when you are not intentionally following the polymerization protocol (which involves controlled color/phase changes at higher temps).*
- *Fix: Reduce temperature and heating rate. If polymerization was not the intended step, discard/restart with closer temperature control.*

*Contamination on Grid*

- *Issue: Debris, residue, or contamination visible across the grid surface.*
- *Cause: Typically due to poor water quality, improper handling, fingerprints, or not cleaning the boat, among other factors.*
- *Fix:*
  - *Keep the instrument consistently cleaned throughout sample thinning to prevent buildup of extra material.*
  - *Always wear gloves when handling grids or related components to avoid contamination from fingerprints/skin oils.*
  - *Ensure water used is ultra-pure. Verify water quality before use, as impurities are a common source of grid contamination.*
  - *Document and inspect all new/empty grids for defects before attaching a sample, to rule out pre-existing issues versus contamination introduced during handling*
